01 · Optuna

Sixty-five trials, one surprise.

Hyperparameter search on the SUPER-ENRICHED corpus ran 65 trials of Bayesian TPE through Optuna, persisted to /tmp/btc_v9_3_cache/optuna_study.db. The composite objective is a weighted blend of UP-side and DOWN-side per-window WR across the deployment threshold band. Trial #65 won at 0.8136.

The trajectory below is the raw value per trial in order of completion. The early bracket sits around 0.79; the search settles into the 0.80 – 0.81 band by trial 14; trial #65 finally cracks above the previous best (#47 at 0.8129). No single trial is dramatically better — the win is the consistency. There are no catastrophic dips after trial 14, which is the real signal that the search converged on a stable regime.

Optuna composite · trial trajectory
65 completed trials · TPE sampler · best = #65 at 0.8136 · y-axis = composite objective

The best params — not what we expected

The winning parameter set is genuinely counter-intuitive. The conventional wisdom on tabular LGB on financial data is: medium-depth trees (6 – 8), moderate num_leaves (31 – 127), small learning_rate, light regularization. Trial #65 picked depth-2 stumps, 118 leaves, heavy bagging at 0.656 with freq=10, and λ2=3.18.

The story that fits: at depth 2 each tree captures a single interaction at most. The model becomes essentially a huge additive ensemble of pairwise interactions. Combine that with feature subsampling (feature_fraction=0.714) and aggressive row bagging, and the result is a model that can't overfit any individual signal — which is exactly the failure mode that haunts prediction-market models trained on shallow horizons.

Surprise · the depth-2 finding

Of the top-5 trials by composite, all five picked max_depth=2. Trial #65 (0.8136), #47 (0.8129), #36 (0.8103), #27 (0.8093), #19 (0.8092) — depth-2 everywhere. That isn't Optuna stumbling into a local optimum; that is the surface itself favouring shallow trees on this corpus.

What this changes about the deploy

The previous v9.2 baseline ran num_leaves=63 per the per-Δ head config. v9.3 with these params is still a single binary head; the difference is the params themselves. Engine-side this is a swap of the LGB booster_file and a config-only feature-list update — no inference-time changes. The deploy story for the drop-in strategy is genuinely boring, which is what we want.

02 · Threshold sweep

The shape of the operating curve.

With the winning params baked in, the threshold sweep traces how per-window WR moves as we tighten or loosen the firing gate. The bands matter more than any single point — they tell us where on the curve a deploy can sit without being one bad week away from collapse.

Two anchor points: the LIVE thresholds (UP 0.72, DOWN 0.20) for an apples-to-apples comparison with v9.2 LIVE, and the 90%-WR thresholds (UP 0.935, DOWN 0.065) found by a target-WR search that asked, "what's the loosest gate that still hits 90%?" The answer at both ends is honest: 90% is reachable, 95%+ isn't.

Honest finding · the ceiling

The walk-forward CV ceiling is ~92 – 93% per-window WR. Every threshold above that drops to zero fires before reaching the WR target. This isn't a search-budget problem — it's a structural limit of what the current feature set can predict at 5m horizon on BTC. Beating it requires either new microstructure features (Tier 4-5 in the next-steps section) or a different prediction target.

The two reachable points become the operating gates for the two deployable strategies in the next section. They aren't picked to look good; they're picked as the loosest gates that hit 90%.

04 · Fire timing

The first two minutes of every window.

Each 5-minute window has 300 seconds of inference. The 90%-WR strategies don't fire uniformly through the window — they fire early, in the first two-to-three minutes after the window opens. The seconds-to-close statistics make this concrete.

For the UP 0.935 strategy: median first-fire is at stc=274 s (so 26 s into the window) and median last-fire is at stc=128 s. The Q25-Q75 band of first-fires sits at 214 – 276 s. The DOWN 0.065 strategy looks similar: first-fire median 258 s, last-fire median 182 s, Q25-Q75 first 226 – 276 s.

Fire-time histogram · 90%-WR strategies
seconds-to-close distribution · UP 0.935 + DOWN 0.065 · first-fire and last-fire bands shown · window opens at stc=300, closes at stc=0

Implication · the tradeable subsegment

For execution policy, this means the tradeable window isn't all 300 seconds. It's the first ~120 – 180 seconds after the candle opens. Fires after that are clustered in the noise band (median last-fire is well past mid-window) and add cost without much edge.

A live engine could enforce a "no-fire after stc < X" guard to skip the tail and reduce trade count without measurably hurting WR. We are not recommending that change as part of v9.3 deploy — the drop-in is supposed to be config-only. But it's a free optimisation lined up for the next iteration.

Why this matters for Polymarket

Settlement velocity on Polymarket 5m markets is non-trivial. Fires near close have less room to fade through, which means more autoredeem-hidden wins and more visible losses. Fires earlier in the window have more time to drift back before settlement — better behaviour from an execution-cost lens, not just a WR lens.

05 · What didn't work

The sequence detour.

Two experiments ran in parallel with the SUPER-ENRICHED training: a path-shape categorical encoding (B) and sequence-derived scalars from 900-tick history (A). Neither cleared the +0.005 composite bar. Net lift across both: +0.0013. Below the noise floor of the sweep.

The hypothesis was reasonable: the 5m windows have rich intra-window dynamics, and a 900-tick lookback ought to add something orthogonal to the per-window aggregates already in the corpus. The result said otherwise — the nested_5m_* aggregates that the ENRICHED tier already computes capture most of the intra-window dynamics that matter at this horizon.

Experiment A

Sequence scalars from 900-tick history

Computed 12 path-statistic features (drift, vol, run-up/run-down, entropy, autocorr) over a sliding 900-tick window and added them to the SUPER corpus. Trained the Optuna-best model with these features included.

Result: marginal composite shift, well within the seed-to-seed noise band of the threshold sweep. The features are picked up by feature importance, but they're mostly redundant with existing aggregates — they re-encode information the model already had.

verdict · rejected · composite lift ≈ +0.001
Experiment B

Path-shape categorical (5-bucket)

Bucketed each window's intra-window path into one of five shapes (up-trend, down-trend, V, inverted-V, choppy) using a heuristic on tick-level returns, then one-hot encoded as four extra features.

Result: also marginal. The path-shape bucket correlates with outcome, but the model can already infer "trend vs chop" from per-window vol and autocorrelation features in the ENRICHED tier.

verdict · rejected · composite lift ≈ +0.0003

Why we're skipping B+ and C

The follow-on experiments queued behind these were B+ (LoRA-style learned hidden state from a small RNN trained on the tick history) and C (1D CNN over the 900-tick window). The expectation now is that both will fail for the same reason: nested_5m_* aggregates already capture per-window dynamics, and 900-tick history doesn't carry additional signal at 5m horizon.

Killing those experiments cleanly frees ~1.5 days of compute for the Tier 4-5 microstructure work, which is the bet that actually has theoretical reason to add new information (order-book imbalance, gamma exposure, VPIN at 60s — things that aren't in the existing aggregates).

Negative result · documented

We're keeping the A + B feature columns archived in btc_seq_features.parquet and v9_3_SEQ_feature_importance.csv for traceability. They don't ship. They aren't part of the deploy. They're documented here so the next person doesn't have to rediscover the dead end.
