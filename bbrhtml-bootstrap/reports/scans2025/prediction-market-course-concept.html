prediction-market AI · internal training guide

Trading binary outcome markets with a stacked ML pipeline.

A grounded, opinionated walkthrough of how the novakash stack predicts 5-minute Polymarket BTC binary outcomes — from raw orderbook ticks through LightGBM boosters, isotonic calibration, per-strategy meta-gates, and a 2-shot FAK execution ladder. Optimised for a sharp ML engineer joining the team or a future self skimming this on a plane.

compiled: 2026-05-18 branch: fix/gate-feature-ordering-gamma host: dev box (RTX 3060) audience: ML eng, week-0 onboard read time: ~75 min deep, ~20 min skim

01The problem space

Polymarket BTC 5-minute binary outcome markets: every five minutes the asset either closed up or down vs the open price. You sell or buy YES/NO shares between ¢1 and ¢99 of payoff. The market resolves in < 5 minutes, you get instant PnL feedback, and the cycle starts again.

What we are actually predicting

For each 5-minute window w with open price popen and close pclose, the outcome is binary:

UP    if p_close > p_open
DOWN  if p_close < p_open
(ties resolve per the Polymarket spec; rare on BTC 5m)

The model emits P(UP | features at tick t). t is parameterised by eval_offset: seconds remaining until the window resolves — not seconds since open. This is a recurring source of confusion; it matters because the signal-to-noise ratio collapses as eval_offset grows (less recent price information; more time for the underlying to move). See hub note #524 for the verification that established this convention against the actual data.

WHY binary, 5m, crypto

High volume. BTC/ETH 5m markets have the deepest CLOB books on Polymarket, sometimes thousands of dollars of YES depth within a 3¢ spread.
Fast resolution. Win/loss feedback in < 5 min — a real PnL loop you can train against in days, not months.
Cleaner labels. The outcome is exogenous (CEX last trade in resolution window). No referee, no subjectivity, no dispute window.

The market microstructure you have to budget around

  • FAK execution. Fill-and-kill: take whatever exists at your cap, cancel the rest. No resting orders. This is the only safe primitive on Polymarket where flicker-fills at bad prices can wreck a 90% WR signal.
  • Partial fills. The CLOB book on the YES side at ¢87 might be $40 deep when you wanted $100. You record the partial, your effective stake is smaller than intended, Kelly maths needs to know.
  • Slippage budget. Strategy declares entry_cap (price floor for fills). The execution layer tries the cap, then cap + 3.14¢, then stops. The realized spread between mid and fill price is your friction tax (~4–6pp of expected edge per #526).
  • Book vs Gamma propagation lag. A freshly-rolled 5m market has valid Gamma metadata seconds before the CLOB books are indexed. The first FAK in that race window 404s with No orderbook exists; we treat it as a benign skip, not an error (BOOK_404_MAX_RETRIES=1).
EXAMPLE — a winning tick

Window 1779117900, T-105s. Engine sees probability_lgb_v9_2 = 0.81, well above the 0.75 UP threshold. CLOB UP-side best ask = 84¢. We FAK at entry_cap=0.89 — fills at the actual book price (84¢), not at the cap. 105 seconds later BTC closes 0.12% up vs open. +$15 PnL on a $100 stake. Per #527 this is the v9.1 win that v9_2_raw_lgb lost on a stricter conviction-vs-price trade-off.

Honest 7-day held-out forward pass at retrain time: +5.92pp weighted accuracy over v9 PROD across five delta heads (#313). Uses Polymarket priceToBeat (Chainlink Streams off-chain) for the open-price baseline — correcting a ~$14.68/window mean-abs drift in the legacy on-chain oracle.

Meta-gates — P(strategy wins this trade)

For each strategy in scope (v9_1_lgb_only, v12_lgb_combo, future v9_2_*), train a LightGBM booster of the strategy’s own gate decisions. The label is “did this trade win?”. The features explicitly exclude the strategy’s own gate-pass booleans (see §3). At serve time, three meta-gate probs land in window_evaluation_traces.surface_json:

probability_v2_meta_gate    -- P(WIN | features) for v2-led decisions
probability_v9_2_meta_gate  -- P(WIN | features) for v9.2-led decisions
probability_v12_meta_gate   -- P(WIN | features) for v12-led decisions
GOTCHA — meta-gate keys are full names

The shortforms mg92, mg2, mg12 do not exist in the JSONB. Use the full probability_v9_2_meta_gate form. signal_evaluations shows 0% for these columns because of a writer bug (PR #536 fix incomplete); the source of truth is window_evaluation_traces.surface_json. memory/reference_meta_gate_data_location.md

Sub-signal extractors (v3 family)

Inside app/v3_composite_scorer.py live the multi-signal fusion components that emit features the v5 boosters consume:

  • v3_composite — weighted fusion in [-1, +1]: ELM, cascade, taker, OI, funding, VPIN, momentum. Per-timescale weight profiles (5m emphasises ELM + VPIN; weekly emphasises funding + momentum).
  • v3_cascade from v3_cascade_estimator.py: cascade FSM state in {CASCADE_UP, CASCADE_DOWN, NO_CASCADE}, with magnitude (v3_cascade_strength), time constant (v3_cascade_tau1), and exhaustion metric.
  • v3_taker, v3_oi, v3_funding — Coinglass-derived flow signals (see §3).

These are NaN-safe at the booster: missing source ⇒ weight redistributes to remaining signals at the composite, missing column at the booster ⇒ LightGBM’s missing-value branch. The latter is the audit #391 trap when it’s silently true rather than rare.

03The feature engineering pyramid

78 features in the v5 column set, in five rough strata: raw market data, engineered micro-features, Coinglass macro, time encodings, and target-encoded cell features. Plus the hard-banned set the meta-gate refuses to even see.

PYRAMID OF FEATURES ▲ / 1 \ cell encoding (target-encoded, walk-fwd) /-----\ / 5 \ time (session bucket, hour, t-band) /---------\ / ~10 \ macro (funding, OI delta, taker ratio, /-------------\ liquidations, long/short pct) / ~20 \ engineered (vpin, delta_pct, delta_binance, /-----------------\ delta_chainlink, regime, v3_composite) / ~40 \ raw (CLOB orderbook, binance ticks, /---------------------\ chainlink, tiingo close, price_std_5m, n_ticks_300s)

Stratum 2 — engineered micro-features

  • vpin: volume-synchronized probability of informed trading, recomputed off the binance ticks in app/vpin_volume_clock.py. Mean / std / range over 60s windows.
  • delta_pct, delta_binance, delta_chainlink, delta_tiingo: relative move vs open price since the window opened, computed per source. source_delta_divergence = |tiingo_vs_binance - chainlink_vs_binance| — flags cross-source confusion.
  • regime_num: discrete state from v3_regime_classifier.py, one of {CASCADE, TRANSITION, NORMAL, CALM}. v9.2 super excludes CALM entirely (n < 15 fires).
  • twap_delta: TWAP vs spot — mean-reversion / momentum proxy.

Stratum 3 — Coinglass macro

From app/v2_coinglass_poller.py → v3_macro_store.py → cached at ticks_coinglass. Funding rate, open-interest delta, taker buy/sell ratio, long/short %, liquidation deltas. Slow-moving (5-10 min latency typical) — used as macro context, not entry trigger.

Stratum 4 — time features

SQL-side bucket of UTC hour from training/queries.py:228-232:

CASE
    WHEN EXTRACT(hour FROM window_end) BETWEEN  0 AND  3 THEN 0  -- Late Asian
    WHEN EXTRACT(hour FROM window_end) BETWEEN  4 AND  8 THEN 1  -- Early Asian
    WHEN EXTRACT(hour FROM window_end) BETWEEN  9 AND 11 THEN 2  -- London

Plus raw hour_of_day, minute_of_hour, day_of_week, and a t_band bucketing of eval_offset (30/60-buckets) consumed by the cell encoder.

Stratum 5 — cell encoding

The per-cell layer is target-encoded by walk-forward folds (no leakage) along the composite key:

cell_key = direction : t_band : regime : session

This is how per-cell WR and per-cell distribution ranges (dist_min, dist_hi) get computed for the “cell pause” gate (#379 + #385). Per-cell overrides on param_overrides_by_cell live or die on whether the cell key matches exactly — no wildcards. The 2026-05-08 audit findings (memory note feedback_runtime_scope_precision) turned into per-cell rows, not a global gate change.

The HARD-BANNED features

Inside training/meta_v2/__init__.py (commit 0f05a4e):

BANNED_FEATURES = frozenset([
    "gate_passed",
    "gate_vpin_passed",
    "gate_delta_passed",
    "gate_cg_passed",
    "gate_twap_passed",
    "gate_timesfm_passed",

    "v2_agrees",
    "v2_high_conf",

WHY ban these

The meta-gate predicts “will the strategy’s call win?”. Its training rows are only decisions that already passed the strategy’s gate stack. Feeding gate_passed as a feature makes the model see the constant 1.0 on every row — useless at best, leakage when paired with other gate-correlated columns. Worse, in evaluation mode the model sees a different distribution (mixed pass/fail) and learns shortcuts that don’t generalise. tests/test_meta_v2_features.py fails-closed if any banned feature sneaks back in.

04The training pipeline

Two canonical entrypoints. One dataset builder, one trainer. Everything else is the audit #391 anti-pattern.

Canonical entrypoints

training.build_dataset      # window-level Parquet (queries.py LATERAL joins)
training.novakash_dataset   # per-tick from signal_evaluations
training.train_lgb_v5       # single wide model, eval_offset as feature
training.train_lgb_v5_optuna # v9.2's variant (37 leaves, depth 7, lr=0.057)
GOTCHA — Schema A vs Schema B trainer choice

There are two trainers; they look interchangeable; they are not. Default to train_lgb_v5.py (78 features, Schema A) for any v9.x retrain. train_lgb_v5_ticks.py takes the Schema-B path that consumes ticks_v2_probability.features JSONB — only 29 features. Using it for a v9.x retrain is the audit #391 trap (silently NaN-stubs the missing 49 columns). Memory note: feedback_schema_a_vs_b_trainer_choice.

Split discipline

70 / 15 / 15 chronological. Never random. The prepare_training_frame function in train_lgb_v5.py sorts by evaluated_at and slices in row-count order. Base rate is computed from train only (audit #0008). The val split is your honest validation; test is held out for promotion measurement.

Audit #391 anti-pattern (silent NaN stubbing)

The old train_v9_1_honest.py shipped this:

for f in V9_FEATURES:
    if f not in train_pool.columns:
        train_pool[f] = np.nan   # silent stub
    train_pool[f] = pd.to_numeric(train_pool[f], errors="coerce")

LightGBM happily trained on constant-NaN columns. min_gain_to_split=0.01 blocked splits, the feature ended up with zero importance, no error, no warning. The v9.1 +5.92pp lift was real on the working feature subset, but ~17 declared features were silent dead weight. Fix: train_lgb_v5.py now distinguishes KNOWN_OPTIONAL_FEATURES from unexpectedly_missing and raises on the latter.

Promotion bar

  • Wilson 95% lower bound on the WR of the held-out test set — never just the point estimate. In relaxation_v12_v9_1.py: WILSON_FLOOR = 0.60, candidate cells need WR ≥ 0.70 AND Wilson_LB ≥ 0.60 AND n ≥ 30.
  • ECE / Brier checks via training/evaluate.py. The iso-refit decision rule (iso_refit_v9_1_v12.py): refit only if ECE improvement ≥ 30% AND Brier improvement ≥ 5% on the honest split.
  • Forward-pass overlap. Honest forward pass on out-of-sample windows that the model has never seen, by strategy not by booster — e.g. v9.1 hits +5.92pp weighted across deltas (60/90/120/180/240) on the 7d holdout per #313.
  • Manual promote. Even when a candidate clears the bar, the deploy is hand-flipped by Billy. See §9.

05The execution layer

A model that says “p=0.81 UP” is a paper PnL until you actually buy YES shares. The execution layer is where realized PnL diverges from theoretical — and where you can give back 4–6pp of edge through bad fills.

The 2-shot FAK ladder

Code: engine/execution/fok_ladder.py (FOKLadder) wrapped by engine/adapters/execution/fak_ladder_executor.py (FAKLadderExecutor). The complete v9.0 algorithm:

read CLOB best_ask (one shot, with 404 retry) if best_ask < PRICE_FLOOR (default 0.30): abort_floor Attempt 1: FAK at entry_cap (e.g. 0.89 for v13_edge, 0.65 for v9 family) if filled → record + done. if zero → wait FOK_INTERVAL_S (default 2s). Attempt 2: FAK at entry_cap + π (π = FOK_PI_BONUS_CENTS = 0.0314) if filled → record + done. if zero → exhausted; strategy moves to Phase 2/3. Phase 2 RFQ: try market-maker RFQ at entry_cap (if FAK_LADDER_ENABLE_RFQ). Phase 3 GTC: DISABLED by default since 2026-04-17 phantom-fill incident.
CASE STUDY — 2026-04-17 phantom-fill incident

The Phase-3 GTC fallback (resting limit at entry_cap + π) was leaving stale orders that filled minutes later at terrible PnL after the signal’s edge had decayed. WR/PnL calculations poisoned. Engine globally disabled GTC: FAK_LADDER_ENABLE_GTC defaults to false; explicit opt-in required per strategy. trade_recorder.py:67 comment + hub note #147.

Why π cents?

3.14¢ is an in-joke bonus — just large enough to skip past a sub-cent flicker bid, small enough not to overpay. The constant is FOK_PI_BONUS_CENTS = 0.0314 in fok_ladder.py and is env-overridable (FOK_PI_BONUS_CENTS).

The proposed epsilon ladder (hub note #526)

The 2-shot algo has known gaps: it doesn’t re-read the book between attempts, and it pays entry_cap on attempt 1 even when best_ask sits 4–6¢ below. The proposal:

best_ask = read_clob_best_ask()
if best_ask >= PRICE_FLOOR_MAX:  # e.g. 0.90
    abort

for attempt in 1..N:                      # N = 4-6
    px = min(best_ask + (attempt - 1) * epsilon, PRICE_FLOOR_MAX)
    submit FAK at px
    if filled: record + break
    sleep(FOK_INTERVAL_S)
    best_ask = read_clob_best_ask()        # re-anchor to live book

Epsilon = Polymarket tick size (0.005 or 0.01 — TBD against exchange spec). Lifts ALL strategies uniformly; no per-strategy retune required. Estimated value: 4pp realized fill improvement on the v9.x family ≈ $4 saved per $1 risked per fill (#526).

Wallet truth vs trades.pnl_usd

The trades table has a pnl_usd column that the strategy scorecard reads. That column is computed at trade-resolution time. There is a separate scripts/ops/wallet_truth.py that hits the actual Polymarket wallet endpoint and reconciles cleared positions. When they disagree the wallet wins — partial fills can land between recording boundaries, and the v9_1_lgb_only scorecard in #523 showed 32W/5L while trades.pnl_usd showed 31W/5L for the same trade set (one WIN row carried a negative pnl_usd).

DRILL — reconcile a day

Pick a single day. Run scripts/ops/wallet_truth.py --hours 24. Pull SELECT strategy_id, SUM(pnl_usd) FROM trades WHERE executed_at::date = X GROUP BY 1. They should agree within rounding. If they don’t, the partial-fill accounting path is the likely culprit (trade_recorder.py). Document the gap.

06The decision & gating architecture

A strategy is not its model. It’s a model + a stack of gates that decide whether the model’s confident call gets to fire. The novakash stack has six layers of these, and turning the wrong knob is the most common way to lose money slowly.

The gate stack — six layers, in order

#GateWhat it checksWhere
1chainlink_freshness Resolution oracle has updated within ~60s. Audit #374. declarative gate in gates YAML
2Conviction probability_lgb_v9_2 ≥ 0.75 (or per-strategy threshold) pre_gate_hook
3N consecutive ticks min_consecutive_pass_ticks: v9.1 uses 3, v9_2_raw_lgb uses 1 gate_params
4Cohort gate Per-(vpin_regime × direction) conviction_x and ticks_n thresholds. v9.2 super uses 8 ticks @0.85 conviction in TRANSITION_UP. v9_2_raw_lgb drops this entirely. gate_params.conviction_x_thresholds
5Sister-pair veto Veto when both members of a pair (e.g. v9_1_cascade_fade_late + v9_cascade_fade_late) oppose, within a 20s agreement window. PR #504. sister_pair_veto in YAML
6Cell pause Per-cell rolling-WR auto-pause. Audits #379 + #385. cell_pause declarative gate
7min/max offset min_offset_sec=45, max_offset_sec=200 (v9.1) — the eval_offset cap. v9.2 is 60–210. gate_params

v9_2_raw_lgb — minimum-gate baseline

By design v9_2_raw_lgb uses only chainlink_freshness + conviction + offset. The point is to isolate the model’s raw alpha — if it survives without the gate stack, the gates are friction; if it doesn’t, the gates are doing real work. #508 introduced it; #537 recommends keeping it (don’t put a meta-gate on top yet — v9.2’s ungated WR is already 99% post-fix, gating only adds cost).

Per-cell overrides

Audit findings frequently surface as “cell X has degraded WR”. The disciplined fix is not a global threshold change — it’s a per-cell override on param_overrides_by_cell, enumerating cell keys exactly. No wildcards. Pair with block_cells dist_min guards. Memory note: feedback_runtime_scope_precision.

CASE STUDY — tiered thresholds (#531) falsified

Tempting idea from the 6–90s vs 151–210s band asymmetry: lower the threshold on late-window ticks (where v9.2 calibrates better) but keep it strict early. 07Calibration as a first-class concern

A LightGBM booster trained with objective=binary emits something close-but-not-equal to P(y=1). Closing that gap is the highest-leverage trick in this stack short of feature engineering. Three methods live in training/calibration.py; pick the right one and you unlock honest staking, honest threshold tuning, and meta-gate inputs that don’t require the meta to relearn the distortion.

Why raw LGB output is not a probability

Boosted-tree binary classifiers tend to be over-confident in the tails and under-confident in the middle when trained with logistic loss + early stopping. The decision-boundary calibration is fine; the per-bin probability calibration is not. v9.2 is currently under-confident: raw p92 = 0.75 maps to realized WR ≈ 0.95; raw 0.65 maps to realized 0.90. Without calibration, Kelly under-bets every confident trade.

Three methods, one serving format

All three produce {x_thresholds, y_thresholds, kind, version, out_of_bounds} and are consumed via np.interp(raw, x, y) at serve time. The booster doesn’t know which method calibrated it — that’s the abstraction win.

MethodMechanismParamsBest forFailure mode
isotonic Non-parametric monotone step function (sklearn IsotonicRegression) ~K breakpoints Big val sets (>5k), arbitrary curve shape Bimodal with small val sets < 1k
platt Logistic on raw: P_cal = σ(a·raw + b) 2 (a, b) Small data, smooth curve Can’t fit S-curves outside sigmoid family
temperature P_cal = σ(logit(raw) / T) 1 (T) Fixing systematic over-/under-confidence Can’t fix non-monotone miscal

Rank-preserving — what iso can and cannot do

Isotonic is monotone non-decreasing. It will never change the order of two predictions: if raw(a) < raw(b) then cal(a) ≤ cal(b). Implications:

  • It cannot manufacture signal. If the model has no edge in a band, iso preserves that. The 211–240s extension zone has WR ~50% — iso would map every raw value there to ~0.5 regardless. That’s why the 210s cap is provably correct, not an artefact of the threshold. #528.
  • It can’t unlock more trades at a fixed threshold. Same rank order ⇒ same fire set.
  • It DOES enable honest threshold-as-WR-target. If cal(p) = WR(p), then setting threshold = 0.85 means “fire when WR ≥ 85%”.
  • It DOES improve Kelly sizing. Honest probabilities → correct optimal stake.

GOTCHA — identity-iso footgun

If the iso JSON file is missing or malformed, the loader (app/isotonic_calibrator.py) falls back to identity (raw = calibrated). This is silent — no exception, just a warning log line like v12 iso not found at <path> — will use identity. The 2026-05 audit found v12’s d=090/120/180/240 boosters all running identity-iso in production (#353 bug 2). When you load a candidate iso, read the warning lines and confirm the file actually parsed. Test: sample some raw probs through it and verify the output differs from the input.

09The discipline & culture

Half the technique here is what you don’t do. The rules below are scar tissue from real incidents, and the deploy pipeline is structured to make the rules enforceable from outside.

The five rules

  1. Manual promote. Every --promote flag is hand-flipped by Billy. Post-2026-04-17 rule. No exceptions, no auto-promote on ECE bar, no “CI promoted it because the test passed”.
  2. Trust code over docs. RUNBOOK and README still reference Railway after the AWS RDS migration. Verify infra by grepping env-var reads, not by quoting docs.
  3. Canonical training entrypoints only. training.build_dataset + training.train_lgb_v5. Everything else is the audit #391 anti-pattern.
  4. Read-only for prod artefacts. No --delete on rsync to v2 box, no S3 key overwrite without a backup, no strategy_configs writes without a journal entry.
  5. Hub notes as long-term memory. Every non-trivial finding goes into the notes table with an ID. Future-self references by ID (#NNN), not by “that thing we figured out last Tuesday”.

The bg-agent pattern

The novakash workflow leans hard on background Claude agents to do bounded research/analysis tasks while the human focuses on decisions. The patterns that work:

  • Bounded scope. One DB to read, one file to write. No commit, no S3 write, no model touch.
  • Verifiable claims. Every claim is grounded in either a file path + line, a SQL query that reproduces, or a hub note ID. Memory snippets are point-in-time observations — verify against current code.
  • Hub notes as deliverable. Findings land in notes with companion HTML/markdown in docs/. The ID is the long-term reference.
  • Falsifiability. If a scan produces a headline-grabbing result, the next step is the longer baseline — not promotion. See #531 (tiered thresholds) for the canonical example.
WHY the no-auto-promote rule

2026-04-17. An auto-promote based on a passing ECE bar pushed a v12 calibration that triggered the GTC phantom-fill incident. The chain was: candidate cleared the offline bar → CI deployed → GTC fallback paid stale prices for hours before anyone noticed → WR/PnL calculations poisoned. Two manual interventions later (rule + GTC default-off), the engine is more boring and the WR is real.

10The 6-week curriculum

A structured onboarding course built around the stack above. Each week has reading, hands-on drills against the local PG snapshot or the DuckDB Parquet pipeline, and a checkpoint you should be able to clear before moving on. By end of week 6 the trainee proposes a new strategy variant offline-verified against real data — the capstone.

WEEK 01

Polymarket primer + math foundations

The mental model: binary outcome markets, CLOB microstructure, FAK execution, slippage budget. The math: Wilson lower bound, ECE, Brier, log-loss. Probability vs payoff vs Kelly.

  • Read: §1 (problem space) of this guide. engine/execution/fok_ladder.py docstring. Hub notes #508 + #524.
  • Drill: compute Wilson LB by hand for (k=27, n=30) and (k=270, n=300). Why are they different at the same point estimate? Reproduce against relaxation_v12_v9_1.py:wilson_lb.

Checkpoint: you can explain why eval_offset is “seconds remaining”, not “seconds since open”, and what breaks if you confuse them.
WEEK 02

Feature engineering on this stack

The 78-feature pyramid. Where each feature comes from, where it’s written, how to inspect it. The sidecar-table trap (#391). The banned-feature list and why.

  • Read: §3 + §4 (training pipeline) of this guide. training/queries.py top-to-bottom. training/train_lgb_v5.py:106-215 (the FEATURE_COLUMNS_V5 list). training/meta_v2/__init__.py.

WEEK 03

End-to-end retrain on the dev box. 70/15/15 chrono split. Honest forward pass. Wilson bar. The audit #391 anti-pattern in detail.

WEEK 04

Calibration, isotonic, ECE/Brier

Why raw LGB isn’t a probability. Isotonic vs Platt vs temperature. The identity-iso footgun. Why iso can’t unlock more trades but can fix staking.

  • Read: §7 of this guide. training/calibration.py full file. training/iso_refit_v9_1_v12.py. Hub notes #353, #392, #528, #536.
  • Drill: fit all three calibrators (iso, platt, temperature) on the same v9.2 raw probs. Compare reliability curves and ECE. Which one would you ship for a small val set (~500 rows)? For 50,000?
  • Drill: simulate the identity-iso footgun. Hand-edit an iso JSON to invalid form, watch isotonic_calibrator.py fall back to identity, confirm the warning log.
  • Drill: with the v9.2 iso candidate (/tmp/v92_iso_candidate_2026-05-18.json), re-stake the last 7 days of v9.2 fires under fractional-Kelly with raw vs calibrated probs. Report the PnL delta.
Checkpoint: you can explain in two sentences why isotonic is rank-preserving and what that implies for “more trades, same WR” (it doesn’t; threshold tuning does).
WEEK 05

Execution + FAK economics

The 2-shot ladder. Why π cents. The proposed epsilon ladder. The 2026-04-17 GTC incident. Wallet truth vs trades.pnl_usd. Partial-fill accounting.

  • Read: §5 of this guide. engine/execution/fok_ladder.py + engine/adapters/execution/fak_ladder_executor.py. Hub notes #147, #526.
  • Drill: pick a day. Pull all v9_2_raw_lgb trades. For each, compute the gap between best_ask at fire time (from strategy_decisions.metadata_json) and the realized fill price. Histogram. Estimate the dollar value the proposed epsilon ladder would have saved.
  • Drill: run scripts/ops/wallet_truth.py --hours 24 for the same day. Compare to SELECT SUM(pnl_usd) FROM trades WHERE strategy_id='v9_2_raw_lgb' AND .... Reconcile any gap.
Checkpoint: you can write the epsilon-ladder pseudocode from memory and defend each parameter choice against the alternative.
WEEK 06

Capstone — propose a new strategy variant

Apply everything. Pick a hypothesis (e.g. “v9.2 late-window calibrated” or “v12 with cell-specific overrides for the under-performing US-session DOWN cell”). Verify offline. Write a hub note. Ship to GHOST.

  • Hypothesis: stated in one sentence with a measurable claim.
  • Offline verification: DuckDB scan against Parquet. Wilson LB ≥ 0.60 on n ≥ 30 fires. ECE not worse by > 0.02 vs the comparison strategy.
  • Hub note: ID-tagged, methodology + data + verdict. Include the contamination check (clean post-fix window only).
  • GHOST deploy: YAML in repo OR strategy_configs row, mode: GHOST, PR open. No LIVE flip.
  • Promotion criteria: stated up-front, in the hub note. “n ≥ 5,000 across 7+ days shadow, ΔWR ≥ +2.0pp vs LIVE comparison, ECE_max not worse by > 0.02” (the v9.1 template per its YAML).
Checkpoint: Billy reviews the hub note, says “yes flip GHOST”, and you flip it — by hand.
